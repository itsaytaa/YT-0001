# Brief: Advisory 0001

## The video

**Title:** If Your Cat Ever Stares At An Empty Corner… DO NOT LOOK
**Format:** "rules horror" instruction tape, 16:9, about 5 minutes, English.
**One-line pitch:** A calm government department explains six directives for when your cat stares at an empty corner. In the end you learn what stands in the corner, and that the cat has been protecting you from it every night.

## Story logic (keep consistent)

1. Cats stare at empty corners. Something unlisted stands there during an "inspection".
2. The cat is not afraid; it is **keeping it in the corner** (directive 1).
3. Light and moving furniture do not help: the corner moves with you (directives 2–3).
4. Saying hello registers you: one more occupant than the census lists (directive 4).
5. If the cat looks at you, the thing is next to you; the narrator slips and admits "we" read your book (directive 5, the slip).
6. The emotional turn: your cat has sat between you and the corner every night for 11 years; tonight you take over (directive 6).
7. Addendum: the Department runs a yearly home inspection, its inspectors stand in corners, and your cat has never let one finish. That is why the cat is listed as a risk.

## Tone rules

- Short sentences, second person, imperative mood, no exclamation marks.
- Exact, slightly absurd numbers (16 hours, case 0411, 2,316 greetings, 4,015 evenings, 112 years).
- One dry joke per directive at most. Never gore, never jump scares, never shouting.
- Dread comes from bureaucratic calm, not from monsters on screen. The inspector is shown clearly only in the addendum.
